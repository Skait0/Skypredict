Reading a prediction

The score on the right is the single most likely result. Do not read too much into it. Even a good model rarely puts more than about 12% on any one scoreline, so it describes the shape of the game rather than forecasting it.

The coloured bar is home win, draw and away win, and the width of each part is its chance.

Double chance covers two outcomes at once. 1X means home win or draw, X2 means draw or away win, and 12 means either team wins with only a draw losing.

Confidence reflects how certain the result itself is, not how safe the tip looks. Most football matches are genuinely close, so low confidence is common and normal.

Where the numbers come from

Every team carries an attack rating and a defence rating fitted from its results, with recent matches weighted more heavily than old ones. Each league has its own scoring baseline, because a Swiss game and a Belgian game do not carry the same goal expectancy.

Those ratings give each side an expected goals figure, which becomes a probability for every possible scoreline. The winner, double chance, over and under, and both teams to score are all read from that one distribution, so the numbers never contradict each other.

What it does not know

Results, and nothing else. It has no knowledge of injuries, suspensions, a new manager, a cup tie three days later, or a team already safe in mid-table with nothing to play for. Treat it as one informed opinion rather than the last word.

Predictions are statistical estimates, not certainties.
